# Build · Shape · Invest cabinet

Shared Three.js 0.180.0 / WebGPU scene in `cabinet.js`, rendered directly into the portfolio's `#hero-cabinet` element and the standalone page's `#stage`. No build step; Three.js dependencies and their MIT license are vendored locally.

Run from the repository root:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Homepage: http://127.0.0.1:8765/index.html
Standalone: http://127.0.0.1:8765/wine-cabinet/

The scene contains exactly four objects per row:

- **build**: product strategy; gtm & narrative; founder instinct; AI fluency.
- **shape**: four procedural art bottles modeled after the user-supplied reference: Dalí (melted amber bottle with a drooping neck), Munch (wavy glass and flowing warm streaks), Picasso (angular stained-glass facets), Mondrian (rectangular leaded primary-color glass). Each includes a bottle neck and cork. Geometry lives in `artist-bottles.js`.
- **invest**: ai infra; healthcare; creativity; sports.

Click **build**, **shape**, or **invest** in the homepage introduction to illuminate and extend the matching shelf (invest maps to the bottom row; its internal collection key remains `back`). Click the word again to retract it and dim the lights. The rail itself is also clickable in both previews.

All twelve bottles can be selected individually once the doors are open. Clicking closed glass opens the doors instead of selecting a bottle behind it. The shelf extends, then the selected bottle comes forward. Selecting another bottle returns the previous one before bringing out the new one. Click the selected bottle again, click empty canvas, press Escape, or use the caption's return button to return everything and restore dim lighting. When closing, bottles settle before racks retract and doors shut. Decorative facet outlines are excluded from picking, so their line hit radius cannot intercept neighboring bottles.

Selecting any bottle reveals a fixed gallery caption below the cabinet, synchronized with the bottle leaving its rack. A reserved area keeps the caption clear of the 3D model without shifting the page. It enlarges the complete build or invest label text, or the artist name for the shape row, and shows the collection plus bottle number. Switching bottles fades the previous caption out before revealing the new one. The caption adapts to light/dark mode, announces its title to screen readers, and includes a keyboard-accessible return button.

Click anywhere on either glass pane, door frame, or handle to open/close; both sides of the glass are interactive. Handles include slightly expanded hit areas. Drag to orbit. Click drawers to open them. With the canvas focused: Enter/Space toggles doors, D toggles drawers, Escape clears the selection, R resets the view. Tab also reaches twelve native bottle controls, visible on keyboard focus. Homepage wheel scrolling is preserved. The cabinet pauses outside the viewport and respects reduced motion. WebGL2 fallback is provided by WebGPURenderer; the active backend is exposed on the container's `data-renderer` attribute.

The transparent scene has no surrounding UI or floor background. It responds to the homepage light/dark theme. The former globe and dune animation have been removed from the homepage. The 12°C display is decorative. Rendering uses PBR materials and a procedural studio environment; door panes use alpha compositing. All model geometry and textures are original procedural assets. Previous Imagegen concept artwork remains archived in assets/; it is not used by the homepage.

The camera uses a fixed 40° field of view and a constant orbit radius during rotation. Space is reserved up front for doors and extended bottles; there is no automatic zoom on rotation or selection. Only viewport resizing resets the responsive camera distance. The caption remains outside the canvas in its reserved reading area.


## Gallery label edition

The eight build/back wine labels use the selected Gallery direction: 01–04 edition number, serif portfolio title, a fine vertical divider, olive (build) or terracotta (back) square, and the exact cellar line `angela's cellar`. The shared print artwork is generated by `gallery-labels.js`. Full-size proof: `label-proof.html`. Archived concept proposals remain unchanged.
